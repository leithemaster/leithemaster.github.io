How this site came to be.

Three things I love, set into one idea: a reading where you turn the cards and find me. This page is the thinking behind it, and the system that holds it together.

  2. 02 The ink

01 · The figure

One person not in uniform.

I made this image to say what a resume can't. A crowd of capable people in matching blue jackets, all looking up, and one person in black. That's me: not louder than anyone, just made differently. A designer who also builds.

Everything else is arranged around him. The ink clears around him, the type sits below him, and the blue of the jackets became the colour of the whole site.

  • He is never cropped out, at any screen size.
  • Nothing covers his face: not ink, not type, not buttons.
  • The photo is darkened to sit under type, never recoloured.

02 · The ink

Because I started with a brush.

Before code there was drawing, and ink is the material that answers the hand. Here it follows your cursor, trails a little behind, and settles like smoke. It is tuned slow on purpose: it should feel like breathing, not like a screensaver.

It's a real fluid simulation running on the GPU, written by hand. The colours are sampled from the jackets in the photo, with a thread of the tarot gold.

03 · The cards

Because I've always loved cards.

Card games got me first: the shuffle, the deal, the second before a card turns over. Then the cards themselves. Frames, suits, numerals, whole little worlds printed in a few square inches. Card design is some of my favourite art there is.

A tarot reading gave the site its shape. It turns cards for the past, the present and the future, one at a time. Here the person being read is me, so learning about my work is also learning who I am.

Why these three

  • I · The Magician

    Because I make magic when I work on sites. A blank file turns into something that moves when you touch it.

  • 0 · The Fool

    Because I enjoy everything and want to start everything. People call that foolish. The Fool is the card of jumping in anyway, and that's how I learn.

  • XVII · The Star

    Because sometimes I give myself the star. It's the card of hope and confidence, and the one I draw when I need the boost.

Anatomy of a card

  1. Frame. A double gilt rule with a star medallion in each corner. Every card, front and back.
  2. Numeral. Roman, in Cormorant Garamond, over a short rule. The Fool is 0, as in every tarot deck.
  3. Emblem. One idea, drawn in line only. The Magician: the wand pointing up and down ("as above, so below") under the infinity sign, with the deck's four suits on the table. The Fool: a ring for his zero, holding the sun, the mountains, a rose and a bindle at the cliff edge, with the leap breaking out through the ring. The Star: one great star and seven small ones.
  4. Title. Cormorant Garamond in spaced capitals.
  5. Position. Past, present or future, in DM Mono.
  6. Back. A compass star in a sunburst, dotted orbits, crescent moons and a fixed field of stars.

04 · The system

The rules that hold it together.

Every colour, size and space on the site comes from these tokens. Nothing is picked by eye on the page.

The mark

A tarot card in miniature: the deck's frame, the star from the Star card, and my initials in the card serif. It is the favicon, the header logo and the signature on everything.

  • Always upright, always on night or card blue.
  • Never smaller than 16px tall; below 24px the inner rule may drop.

Colour

  • Night#090a12The stage. Every page sits on it.
  • Ultramarine#2f43d6From the jackets. Ink and fills only, never text on night (2.7:1).
  • Card blue#0b1240Card stock. The deck and the mark.
  • Gilt#c9a24aLine work on cards. 7.4:1 on card blue.
  • Bone#f1ece2Body text. 16.8:1 on night.
  • Ash#a9a6b8Labels and quiet text. 8.3:1 on night.
  • Paper#ebe4d6The resume's light stock. Ink on it is 13.1:1.
  • Old gilt#8a6a24Rules and ornament on paper. Not for text.

Type

Liem Nguyen

Labels, dates, stacks and navigation. Small, uppercase where it labels, tabular numbers where it counts.

Voice

  • First person, plain, short sentences.
  • Name real things: companies, years, tools, outcomes.
  • The tarot is the frame, never the joke. No fortune-teller puns.
  • No "passionate", "innovative" or "rockstar".

Access

  • All text passes WCAG AA; most passes AAA.
  • Cards are real buttons with spoken labels; the drawings are decoration.
  • Reduced motion: cards turn instantly and the ink stops drifting.
  • The resume prints as plain black on white, so applicant tracking systems read it cleanly.
